The Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA), in collaboration with the NASA, ASA, ESA, DLR and CNES worked on the Martian Moon eXploration (MMX) mission for the past 8 years with the objective of sending a spacecraft toward Phobos, the largest Martian moon. 

While the launch is scheduled on October 20th from Tanegashima Island (Japan), arrival in the Mars-moon system is expected in 2027 with start of operation (flybys, landings, sampling, etc.). MMX spacecraft journey back to Earth will be in 2030 with capsule recover on Earth expected in 2031 in Woomera (Australia).

The main objective: sampling up to 10 grams of materials from Phobos surface with potential Martian materials.

Analysis of such samples along with all distant observations made during flybys would allow us to improve our understanding on the Evolution of the Inner and Outer Solar System, along with resolving the mystery around the formation of both Phobos and Deimos (captured asteroids or products of a large impact). 

This would be the 3rd sample return mission led by JAXA and the first one to retrieve materials from the Martian system with potential materials from Mars.



More details on the mission are available on this website: https://www.mmx.jaxa.jp/en/
